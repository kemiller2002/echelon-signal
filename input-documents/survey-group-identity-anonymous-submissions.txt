Survey Engine – Survey Group Identity and Anonymous Submission Model

Purpose

This document defines how survey groups, survey instances, administrator identity mapping, and anonymous submissions should work.

The design goal is to keep the survey engine focused on survey instances and submissions rather than storing personal identity information.

1. Core Principle

The survey engine identifies survey instances, not people.

The administrator is responsible for knowing which person is associated with each identified survey instance.

The survey system should not need to store names, email addresses, employee IDs, or other personal identifiers in the survey URL.

2. Survey Group

A SurveyGroup represents a set of related survey instances.

Recommended core fields:

SurveyGroup
    SurveyGroupId
    ExpectedSurveyCount
    SurveyInstances[]

ExpectedSurveyCount is defined by the administrator when the group is created.

3. Survey Instance

Each survey sent to a respondent has its own instance identity.

SurveyInstance
    SurveyInstanceId
    SurveyGroupId
    SurveyIdentifier
    TemplateVersion
    TemplateFingerprint

4. Administrator Responsibility

The administrator maintains the external mapping between SurveyInstanceId and person.

This mapping does not need to live inside the survey engine.

The survey engine only needs the instance identity.

5. Identified Survey Flow

Invitation URL contains:

SurveyInstanceId
SurveyGroupId
SurveyIdentifier / Template Reference

Completed submission contains:

SurveyInstanceId
SurveyGroupId
EncodedAnswers
Template Reference
Encoding Version
Integrity Information

The administrator matches SurveyInstanceId to the person using their own external mapping.

6. Anonymous Survey Requirement

Anonymous surveys must prevent the completed submission from being linked back to the original invitation.

The original SurveyInstanceId may be needed while the survey is being distributed or completed, but it must not survive into the returned anonymous submission.

Core anonymity invariant:

In anonymous mode, no identifier capable of linking the returned submission to the original invitation may survive into the completed URL.

This includes:
- SurveyInstanceId
- Reversible tokens derived from SurveyInstanceId
- Hidden metadata that preserves the mapping
- Other stable invitation identifiers

7. Anonymous Submission Identity

Anonymous submissions should use a new identifier that is not linkable to the invitation.

Recommended field:

AnonymousSubmissionId

The completed anonymous submission contains:

AnonymousSubmissionId
SurveyGroupId
EncodedAnswers
Template Reference
Encoding Version
Integrity Information

The original SurveyInstanceId is removed before the final submission URL is produced.

8. Why Use AnonymousSubmissionId

Using no identifier at all would maximize anonymity, but it would make it difficult to:
- Distinguish multiple anonymous submissions
- Detect duplicate imports
- Maintain submission records
- Count unique returned artifacts

A new unlinkable AnonymousSubmissionId preserves these operational capabilities without revealing which invitation produced the submission.

9. Anonymous Group Behavior

The SurveyGroupId remains in the anonymous submission.

This allows the administrator to aggregate anonymous responses by group.

Example:

SurveyGroupId = G55
ExpectedSurveyCount = 20
Received anonymous submissions = 17

The administrator can know that 17 responses were received for group G55, but cannot determine which original invitations produced those 17 responses.

10. Privacy Tradeoff

Under the strongest anonymous model, the administrator cannot reliably determine which specific invitees have not responded.

They can know:

Expected = 20
Received = 17

They cannot know which three people are missing if the returned submissions are truly unlinkable to the invitations.

11. Identity Modes

Recommended domain model:

IdentityMode
    IdentifiedInstance
    AnonymousInstance

IdentifiedInstance:
- Submission preserves SurveyInstanceId
- Administrator can map submission to person

AnonymousInstance:
- Submission removes SurveyInstanceId
- Submission generates AnonymousSubmissionId
- Administrator can group/count responses
- Administrator cannot link submission to invitee

12. Suggested Submission Shapes

Identified submission:

SurveySubmission
    SurveyInstanceId
    SurveyGroupId
    TemplateReference
    ResponseEncodingVersion
    EncodedAnswers
    IntegrityValue

Anonymous submission:

AnonymousSurveySubmission
    AnonymousSubmissionId
    SurveyGroupId
    TemplateReference
    ResponseEncodingVersion
    EncodedAnswers
    IntegrityValue

13. What Should Not Be in the URL

Unless there is a compelling requirement, do not include:
- Name
- Email address
- Employee number
- Username
- Phone number
- Human-readable subject identity
- Other personally identifying data

If identity is required, the administrator should maintain it outside the survey engine.

14. Survey Group Counts

The group should support at least:

ExpectedSurveyCount

After administrator import, the receiving system may derive:

ReceivedSubmissionCount

ReceivedSubmissionCount is derived from imported submissions and does not need to be stored in the respondent-facing URL.

15. Duplicate Detection

For identified submissions, duplicate detection may use:

SurveyInstanceId
plus
SubmissionHash

For anonymous submissions, duplicate detection may use:

AnonymousSubmissionId
plus
SubmissionHash

A SubmissionHash can be calculated by the administrator-side import system and does not necessarily need to be stored in the URL.

16. Administrator Import Behavior

Identified import:

Receive URL
-> Read SurveyInstanceId
-> Read SurveyGroupId
-> Resolve template
-> Decode answers
-> Associate instance with administrator's person mapping
-> Score/report

Anonymous import:

Receive URL
-> Read AnonymousSubmissionId
-> Read SurveyGroupId
-> Resolve template
-> Decode answers
-> Do not attempt to resolve original respondent
-> Aggregate/score/report

17. Privacy Boundary

Administrator system:
    Person <-> SurveyInstanceId mapping

Survey engine:
    SurveyInstanceId / AnonymousSubmissionId
    SurveyGroupId
    Template
    Answers
    Results

This keeps identity concerns outside the core survey engine.

18. Template or Group Configuration

The immutable survey template or survey-group configuration should specify the identity mode.

IdentityMode = IdentifiedInstance

or

IdentityMode = AnonymousInstance

This behavior must be fixed before survey distribution.

19. Anonymous Conversion Point

Recommended sequence:

Anonymous Invitation
-> Respondent completes survey
-> Validate survey
-> Generate new AnonymousSubmissionId
-> Remove SurveyInstanceId
-> Encode final submission URL
-> Return/send URL to administrator

The final URL must be tested to ensure the invitation identity does not remain anywhere in the payload.

20. Random Anonymous Identifier

AnonymousSubmissionId should be generated using a sufficiently large random identifier.

It should not be:
- Sequential
- Derived from SurveyInstanceId
- Derived from respondent identity
- Reversible
- Predictable

21. Strong Anonymous Invariant

AnonymousSubmissionId cannot be deterministically derived from SurveyInstanceId.

This prevents administrators or other parties from reconstructing the link.

22. Reporting Implication

Reporting should understand whether a group is identified or anonymous.

For anonymous groups, reports must not imply respondent identity exists.

Group-level reporting may include:
- Number expected
- Number received
- Completion rate
- Aggregate scores
- Section distributions
- Group recommendations

But not respondent-level identity.

23. Core Requirements

1. Every survey instance has a SurveyInstanceId.
2. Related survey instances share a SurveyGroupId.
3. The administrator defines ExpectedSurveyCount for the group.
4. The administrator maintains person-to-instance mapping externally.
5. Personal identity should not be embedded in the survey URL.
6. Identified submissions retain SurveyInstanceId.
7. Anonymous submissions remove SurveyInstanceId.
8. Anonymous submissions receive a new unlinkable AnonymousSubmissionId.
9. Anonymous submissions retain SurveyGroupId for grouping and aggregation.
10. AnonymousSubmissionId must not be derived from the invitation instance ID.
11. No hidden or reversible invitation identifier may survive into an anonymous submission.
12. The administrator can count anonymous submissions but cannot link them back to invitees.
13. IdentityMode must be explicit.
14. Anonymous behavior must be deterministic and testable.
15. Duplicate detection may use submission identity and submission hashes after import.

24. Canonical Identity Model

Identified:

Administrator:
    Person <-> SurveyInstanceId

Submission:
    SurveyInstanceId
    SurveyGroupId
    Answers

Anonymous:

Invitation:
    SurveyInstanceId
    SurveyGroupId

Submission:
    AnonymousSubmissionId
    SurveyGroupId
    Answers

No link between AnonymousSubmissionId and SurveyInstanceId is retained or recoverable by the survey engine.

25. Guiding Principle

The survey engine should know which survey artifact it is processing without needing to know who the respondent is.

For identified surveys, the administrator owns identity mapping.

For anonymous surveys, the completed submission intentionally breaks the link to the invitation while preserving enough anonymous identity to support grouping, counting, deduplication, scoring, and reporting.
