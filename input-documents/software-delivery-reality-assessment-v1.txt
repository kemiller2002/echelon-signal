ECHELON SIGNAL
SOFTWARE DELIVERY REALITY ASSESSMENT (SDRA) - V1 DESIGN

Status: Candidate assessment design / pilot instrument
Version: 0.1.0-draft
Added: 2026-09-23

Purpose
=======

The Software Delivery Reality Assessment (SDRA) is a multi-rater assessment battery intended to determine how a software organization actually operates, independently of the methodology label it uses.

Core question:

    "What operating model does the observed behavior resemble?"

The assessment deliberately separates:

1. Claimed methodology
2. Observed operating behavior
3. Role utilization
4. Engineering/delivery maturity
5. Methodology congruence
6. Cross-role perception differences

The assessment MUST NOT assume that:

- a team using Scrum terminology actually operates as Scrum,
- a ticket board establishes Kanban,
- a project manager makes a team Waterfall,
- a Scrum Master makes a team Scrum,
- frequent releases alone make an organization Agile,
- a methodology classification is an evaluation of whether the organization is good or bad.

The output is descriptive evidence about operating behavior, not certification.

This is an initial instrument design. Thresholds, prototype vectors, item wording, scale structure, and interpretation rules are hypotheses to validate. They MUST be treated as experimental until supported by characterization, pilot data, reliability/validity analysis, and revision evidence.

Reference Basis
===============

The behavioral model should remain traceable to primary reference material where applicable:

- The Scrum Guide, official current version: November 2020.
- The Kanban Guide, official current version reviewed for this draft: May 2025.
- Manifesto for Agile Software Development and its principles.

Scrum evidence used by the assessment includes, among other things:

- cross-functional and self-managing Scrum Teams,
- Product Owner accountability for Product Backlog ordering,
- Developer ownership of Sprint planning/adaptation and quality through Definition of Done,
- Scrum Master responsibility for establishing Scrum and improving team effectiveness,
- fixed-length Sprints,
- inspection and adaptation,
- a usable Increment meeting Definition of Done.

Kanban evidence used by the assessment includes, among other things:

- an explicit Definition of Workflow,
- active management of items,
- explicit WIP control,
- pull based on capacity,
- management of aging/blocked work,
- continual workflow improvement,
- WIP, throughput, work-item age, and cycle-time measures.

The assessment also uses general behavioral dimensions such as handoffs, governance gates, planning commitment, customer feedback, product orientation, and continuous delivery. Those dimensions are diagnostic constructs for SDRA and MUST NOT be misrepresented as official Scrum/Kanban definitions.

Battery Structure
=================

SDRA should be implemented as a battery rather than one monolithic form.

Battery components:

SDRA-CTX
    Context and claimed-methodology component.
    Unscored.
    Captures non-PII operating context.

SDRA-CORE
    Core observed-practices instrument.
    60 behavioral items.
    Completed by all respondent roles.

Conditional role modules:

SDRA-SM
    Scrum Master / Agile facilitator utilization
    8 items

SDRA-QA
    QA / test utilization
    8 items

SDRA-DEV
    Developer utilization
    8 items

SDRA-PM
    Project manager / delivery coordinator utilization
    8 items

SDRA-PROD
    Product Owner / product-management utilization
    8 items

A respondent may select more than one role when they actually perform multiple roles. Every selected role module SHOULD be administered so dual-role patterns such as Scrum-Master-plus-Project-Manager can be observed rather than hidden.

Leadership respondents who do not perform one of the role modules complete SDRA-CTX + SDRA-CORE.

No PII
======

The assessment MUST NOT request:

- name,
- email,
- employee ID,
- manager name,
- team member names,
- customer names,
- free-text descriptions containing identifiable people.

Permitted contextual categories include:

- respondent role category,
- team-size band,
- organization-size band,
- product/project/team scope type,
- claimed methodology,
- release-frequency band,
- tenure-with-team band,
- industry category if desired and privacy-safe.

Small-role cohorts MUST obey Signal anonymous-reporting thresholds.

Observation Window
==================

Respondents should answer based on recent normal behavior, not ideal process.

Default instruction:

"Answer based on what usually happened during the last eight weeks. If your team works in iterations, use at least the last three completed iterations when that gives a more representative view. Answer what actually happens, not what the documented process says should happen."

Response Scale
==============

Core and role items use a five-point behavioral-frequency scale:

0 = Never or almost never
1 = Rarely
2 = Sometimes
3 = Often
4 = Almost always

Special states:

DontKnow
NotObserved
NotApplicable where semantically valid
Unanswered

Special states MUST NOT collapse to zero.

No agree/disagree wording is preferred for behavioral items because frequency wording makes the requested evidence more concrete.

Core Methodology Dimensions
===========================

Every primary dimension is scored 0-100.

Important: direction is descriptive. A higher score is NOT automatically "better."

D01 PLAN_COMMITMENT
    High = more scope/requirements/plan fixed before implementation.
    Low = more emergent planning.

D02 CHANGE_RESPONSIVENESS
    High = new evidence can alter priorities/scope through normal operating mechanisms.

D03 TIMEBOX_DISCIPLINE
    High = consistent short-cycle/timeboxed planning, goal, inspection, and completion behavior.

D04 FLOW_PULL_DISCIPLINE
    High = explicit workflow, WIP control, pull/capacity signals, and active flow management.

D05 CROSS_FUNCTIONAL_OWNERSHIP
    High = stable team owns value end-to-end with few specialist handoffs.

D06 HANDOFF_RELIANCE
    High = work moves through specialist queues/phases before becoming usable.

D07 QUALITY_INTEGRATION
    High = quality is built into normal team delivery rather than downstream verification.

D08 EXTERNAL_GATING
    High = progress/release/change depends strongly on approvals outside the delivery team.

D09 EMPIRICAL_LEARNING
    High = actual outcomes, working software, feedback, and retrospection routinely change plans/process.

D10 TEAM_AUTONOMY
    High = the team determines execution details and adapts its work locally.

D11 DELIVERY_CONTINUITY
    High = software can move to production/releasable state frequently without large stabilization batches.

D12 PRODUCT_ORIENTATION
    High = stable product/value ownership, continuous prioritization, and outcome-based decision making.

Core Items
==========

D01 - Plan Commitment

CORE-001 Major requirements are expected to be substantially defined before implementation begins.
CORE-002 Release scope is committed well before the work needed to deliver it is complete.
CORE-003 Changes discovered after implementation begins normally require formal change approval.
CORE-004 Design, build, test, and release milestones are managed against an upfront baseline plan.
CORE-005 Delivery success is judged primarily by conformance to the original scope/schedule plan.

D02 - Change Responsiveness

CORE-006 New customer or operational evidence can change upcoming priorities through the normal process.
CORE-007 The team can clarify or renegotiate planned scope when it learns something important.
CORE-008 Findings from implementation regularly change what the team plans to do next.
CORE-009 Stakeholder feedback can alter priorities without waiting for a major project phase boundary.
CORE-010 Planning is revisited often enough that newly learned information can materially affect near-term work.

D03 - Timebox Discipline

CORE-011 Work is organized in consistent short iterations or timeboxes.
CORE-012 Each iteration has a shared outcome or goal rather than only a collection of assigned tasks.
CORE-013 Planning, inspection/review, and improvement happen on a consistent iteration cadence.
CORE-014 The team aims to produce usable, integrated work by the end of each iteration.
CORE-015 Work that has not met the team's completion/quality criteria is not counted as finished merely because the iteration ended.

D04 - Flow/Pull Discipline

CORE-016 The team has an explicit shared definition of how work moves from started to finished.
CORE-017 The amount of work in progress is explicitly controlled.
CORE-018 New work is normally started when capacity becomes available rather than simply when it is assigned.
CORE-019 The team uses flow evidence such as cycle time, throughput, work-item age, or equivalent measures.
CORE-020 Aging, blocked, or stalled work is actively reviewed and managed.

D05 - Cross-Functional Ownership

CORE-021 The stable delivery team collectively has most skills needed to turn an idea into usable software.
CORE-022 Product, development, and quality work happens collaboratively rather than as separate sequential departments.
CORE-023 People are generally kept with a stable team rather than reassigned among projects whenever demand changes.
CORE-024 The team remains responsible for work until it is usable/releasable rather than stopping at a specialist handoff.
CORE-025 Dependencies on people outside the team are exceptions rather than the normal path for completing ordinary work.

D06 - Handoff Reliance

CORE-026 Work commonly waits in queues between analysis, development, QA, security, operations, or release functions.
CORE-027 One function completes an artifact or phase and formally hands it to another function to continue.
CORE-028 Development work is commonly considered complete before downstream testing begins.
CORE-029 A separate release or operations function must normally take ownership before software can be delivered.
CORE-030 Progress is commonly tracked by completion of functional phases such as requirements, development, testing, and release.

D07 - Quality Integration

CORE-031 Completion/quality expectations are known before implementation is considered finished.
CORE-032 Developers routinely verify their own work rather than relying on QA to discover ordinary defects.
CORE-033 QA/test expertise participates early enough to influence examples, acceptance criteria, or design.
CORE-034 Automated tests and other quality checks run as part of the routine delivery pipeline.
CORE-035 Defects found during delivery are normally resolved within the same working flow rather than accumulated for a later test/fix phase.

D08 - External Gating

CORE-036 Work commonly requires approval outside the delivery team before it can move to the next major stage.
CORE-037 Releases commonly require formal signoff, a release board, or a comparable external gate.
CORE-038 Material scope changes normally require approval from management/governance outside the team/product owner.
CORE-039 Formal phase or milestone gates determine when the team may proceed.
CORE-040 Functional signoffs are required before work is considered complete even when the delivery team believes it is usable.

D09 - Empirical Learning

CORE-041 Stakeholders regularly inspect working software or actual outcomes rather than relying primarily on status reports.
CORE-042 Retrospectives or equivalent improvement reviews result in observable changes to how the team works.
CORE-043 Delivery/product decisions use evidence from actual results rather than only forecasts and plan completion.
CORE-044 The team intentionally runs small experiments or incremental changes to reduce uncertainty.
CORE-045 When results differ from assumptions, plans or working methods are changed promptly.

D10 - Team Autonomy

CORE-046 The team decides how to perform the work needed to achieve its near-term goal.
CORE-047 Developers/team members participate directly in selecting and planning the work they can complete.
CORE-048 Day-to-day work is not primarily assigned person-by-person by a manager or coordinator.
CORE-049 The team can make ordinary technical and execution tradeoffs without seeking external approval.
CORE-050 Team members can reorganize their work when conditions change without waiting for a manager to rewrite the task plan.

D11 - Delivery Continuity

CORE-051 Completed software can be released independently of a large scheduled batch when the organization chooses to do so.
CORE-052 Build/test/deployment automation supports moving changes toward production routinely.
CORE-053 The team integrates changes continuously enough to avoid long-lived stabilization periods.
CORE-054 A separate code-freeze/regression phase is not normally required to make a release safe.
CORE-055 Production/user feedback can reach the team soon enough to influence subsequent delivery decisions.

D12 - Product Orientation

CORE-056 Teams are organized around persistent products/services/value streams more than temporary projects.
CORE-057 A clearly accountable product decision-maker can continuously reorder upcoming work.
CORE-058 Success is discussed in terms of customer/business outcomes in addition to scope and schedule delivery.
CORE-059 Discovery and delivery interact continuously rather than discovery ending before delivery begins.
CORE-060 Funding/planning/ownership supports continued product evolution after an individual project or release ends.

Role Modules
============

SDRA-SM - Scrum Master / Agile Facilitator

SM-001 The Scrum Master/facilitator coaches the team to manage its own work.
    subscale: Enablement

SM-002 The Scrum Master/facilitator helps the organization remove impediments the team cannot remove itself.
    subscale: Enablement

SM-003 Retrospective learning is turned into concrete changes with help from the Scrum Master/facilitator.
    subscale: Improvement

SM-004 The Scrum Master/facilitator helps stakeholders and the team improve collaboration rather than acting mainly as a status intermediary.
    subscale: Improvement

SM-005 The Scrum Master/facilitator spends substantial effort scheduling ceremonies, collecting status, and maintaining process administration.
    subscale: CeremonyAdministration

SM-006 The Scrum Master/facilitator is held responsible for enforcing delivery dates or individual task completion.
    subscale: ProjectControlSubstitution

SM-007 The Scrum Master/facilitator assigns work or directs day-to-day execution.
    subscale: ProjectControlSubstitution

SM-008 The Scrum Master/facilitator simultaneously performs a project-manager or line-manager role for the same team's ordinary delivery work.
    subscale: RoleOverlap

SDRA-QA - QA / Test

QA-001 QA/test specialists participate before implementation is complete.
    subscale: EmbeddedQuality

QA-002 QA/test expertise helps shape acceptance criteria, examples, test strategy, or design.
    subscale: EmbeddedQuality

QA-003 Quality is treated as the responsibility of the whole delivery team.
    subscale: SharedOwnership

QA-004 Routine regression/verification is substantially automated and part of normal delivery.
    subscale: AutomationIntegration

QA-005 Completed development normally waits for a QA phase or QA queue before it can progress.
    subscale: DownstreamGate

QA-006 QA approval outside the development team is normally required before release.
    subscale: DownstreamGate

QA-007 QA is expected to discover defects that developers could reasonably have detected before handoff.
    subscale: QualitySpecialization

QA-008 Testing occurs primarily after the planned development work for a feature/release is complete.
    subscale: LateTesting

SDRA-DEV - Developer

DEV-001 Developers help decide how much work the team can take on and how it will be implemented.
    subscale: SelfManagement

DEV-002 Developers collaborate directly with product and QA/test rather than communicating primarily through coordinators.
    subscale: CrossFunctionalCollaboration

DEV-003 Developers remain responsible for work through verification and usable/releasable state.
    subscale: EndToEndOwnership

DEV-004 Developers routinely participate in production/support learning for the software they build.
    subscale: EndToEndOwnership

DEV-005 Developers are frequently reassigned among projects according to utilization or resource demand.
    subscale: ResourcePoolBehavior

DEV-006 Developer work is considered complete when coding is finished and the item is handed to another function.
    subscale: HandoffSpecialization

DEV-007 Daily tasks are commonly assigned to individual developers by someone outside the developer group.
    subscale: DirectedExecution

DEV-008 Developers participate in discovery/refinement and can influence scope or solution choices before coding begins.
    subscale: ProductCollaboration

SDRA-PM - Project Manager / Delivery Coordinator

PM-001 The project manager primarily coordinates dependencies, external commitments, budget, or governance without directing the team's daily execution.
    subscale: CoordinationGovernance

PM-002 The project manager helps remove cross-team/organizational constraints that the delivery team cannot resolve itself.
    subscale: CoordinationGovernance

PM-003 The project manager is the central owner of detailed scope, schedule, and delivery-plan conformance.
    subscale: PlanControl

PM-004 The project manager assigns or redistributes day-to-day work among team members.
    subscale: TeamControl

PM-005 Material changes to planned work normally require project-manager approval or escalation.
    subscale: PlanControl

PM-006 Team status primarily flows upward through the project manager rather than being visible from working results/flow.
    subscale: StatusIntermediation

PM-007 The project manager performs responsibilities the organization also describes as Scrum Master responsibilities.
    subscale: RoleOverlap

PM-008 The project manager's role is mainly necessary because delivery crosses many organizational, vendor, governance, or contractual boundaries.
    subscale: DependencyCoordination

SDRA-PROD - Product Owner / Product Management

PROD-001 A clearly accountable product decision-maker can reorder the backlog when evidence changes.
    subscale: PriorityAuthority

PROD-002 Stakeholders who want priority changes normally work through that accountable product decision-maker rather than assigning work directly to the team.
    subscale: PriorityAuthority

PROD-003 Product decisions are tied to customer/user/business outcomes rather than only project scope completion.
    subscale: OutcomeOrientation

PROD-004 Product/stakeholder feedback is gathered frequently enough to alter upcoming work.
    subscale: FeedbackLoop

PROD-005 The product decision-maker is expected to obtain committee/management approval for ordinary backlog reprioritization.
    subscale: ConstrainedAuthority

PROD-006 The backlog mainly represents execution of a scope already committed in a project plan.
    subscale: ProjectPlanDominance

PROD-007 Discovery of customer/problem/solution evidence continues while delivery is underway.
    subscale: ContinuousDiscovery

PROD-008 The product role has authority over priority but does not assign developers' detailed execution tasks.
    subscale: ProductTeamBoundary

Scoring
=======

1. Item scoring

Frequency values map directly:

Never          = 0
Rarely         = 1
Sometimes      = 2
Often          = 3
AlmostAlways   = 4

Special states are excluded from numeric means.

2. Individual dimension score

For each D01-D12:

    DimensionScore = mean(answered item values) / 4 * 100

At least 3 of the 5 dimension items MUST have a numeric answer for the dimension to be Available.

3. Cohort aggregation

First compute respondent-level dimension scores.

Then compute role-cohort scores.

For organizational/team methodology classification, the default SHOULD give equal weight to each sufficiently represented role cohort rather than allowing the largest role population to dominate the result.

Example:

    Developer cohort = 40 people
    QA cohort = 7 people
    PM cohort = 4 people
    Product cohort = 5 people

The default organization profile is the mean of available role-cohort profiles, not the raw mean of all 56 respondents.

A configurable alternative may be allowed, but the aggregation policy MUST be explicit and versioned.

4. Privacy

Role-cohort reporting is suppressed below the configured anonymous minimum.

Overall classification may use all anonymous contributions when the overall privacy threshold is satisfied, but MUST NOT expose suppressed role-level cells through totals/differences.

5. Coverage

Report separately:

- ItemCoverage
- DimensionCoverage
- RoleCohortCoverage
- RespondentCount
- SuppressedCohortCount

Do not call a simple coverage percentage statistical confidence.

6. Dispersion / perception disagreement

For each dimension, calculate cross-role dispersion when privacy thresholds permit.

High dispersion SHOULD produce a perception-gap finding rather than being silently averaged away.

Methodology Archetype Classifier
================================

Claimed methodology is NOT an input to this classifier.

Classification is based on D01-D12.

Initial prototype vectors are hypotheses for pilot testing, not validated norms.

Dimension order:

D02 Change Responsiveness
D03 Timebox Discipline
D04 Flow/Pull Discipline
D05 Cross-Functional Ownership
D06 Handoff Reliance
D07 Quality Integration
D08 External Gating
D09 Empirical Learning
D10 Team Autonomy
D11 Delivery Continuity
D12 Product Orientation

Prototype vectors (0-100):

ScrumLike
    [35, 80, 90, 45, 90, 20, 85, 20, 90, 85, 70, 90]

KanbanLike
    [25, 90, 25, 95, 75, 25, 80, 20, 90, 80, 90, 85]

ScrumbanLike
    [30, 90, 75, 90, 85, 20, 85, 20, 90, 85, 90, 90]

WaterfallLike
    [95, 20, 10, 10, 25, 90, 25, 90, 20, 20, 15, 25]

StageGateHybrid
    [80, 45, 55, 30, 45, 70, 50, 80, 45, 40, 35, 50]

ContinuousProductFlow
    [25, 95, 55, 85, 90, 15, 95, 15, 95, 90, 100, 100]

ProjectizedIterative
    [75, 50, 75, 35, 50, 65, 55, 70, 55, 40, 45, 40]

ReactiveInterruptDriven
    [20, 85, 15, 25, 45, 55, 35, 25, 30, 35, 50, 30]

Similarity:

    Similarity(archetype)
      = 100 - weightedMean(abs(observedDimension - archetypeDimension))

Initial v1 uses equal dimension weights unless pilot evidence supports another weighting.

The output MUST preserve all archetype similarities rather than only the top label.

Classification interpretation (pilot thresholds)
=================================================

Strong single-profile evidence:
    highest similarity >= 75
    AND margin over second highest >= 8

Primary-with-secondary influence:
    highest similarity >= 68
    AND margin over second highest >= 5

Hybrid:
    top two similarities >= 65
    AND absolute margin < 5

Mixed / Indeterminate:
    insufficient coverage
    OR highest similarity < 65
    OR profile contains material contradictions that violate the chosen archetype's required signals

These thresholds MUST be versioned and treated as experimental.

Archetype Required Signals
==========================

Prototype similarity alone is insufficient.

Strong ScrumLike classification also requires, initially:

- D03 Timebox Discipline >= 60
- D05 Cross-Functional Ownership >= 55
- D09 Empirical Learning >= 60
- D10 Team Autonomy >= 55
- CORE-014 and CORE-015 coverage sufficient to evaluate usable-completed iteration behavior

Strong KanbanLike classification also requires, initially:

- D04 Flow/Pull Discipline >= 60
- CORE-016, CORE-017, CORE-018 covered
- evidence of explicit WIP control, not merely a visual board

Strong WaterfallLike classification does not require every practice, but high similarity should normally be supported by multiple independent signals across plan commitment, handoff reliance, and/or external gating.

If similarity is high but required signals fail, return:

    ArchetypeResemblanceWithMissingCoreSignals

rather than falsely asserting strong framework alignment.

Claimed-versus-Observed Congruence
=================================

Claimed methodology is compared only after observed classification.

Example statuses:

Aligned
    Claimed family corresponds to highest observed archetype and observed evidence is strong.

PartiallyAligned
    Claimed family is among strong observed influences but another profile is equally/near-equally present.

BehavioralMismatch
    A different archetype is strongly supported and the claimed family is not among the dominant observed profiles.

Indeterminate
    Observed evidence is insufficient or mixed.

Do not use accusatory language.

Preferred example:

    "The organization identifies this team as Scrum. Observed practices show a stronger stage-gated/projectized profile, particularly in handoffs, approval gates, and plan control."

Avoid:

    "You are not really Agile."

Diagnostic Pattern Flags
========================

These are derived descriptive patterns, not methodology classifications.

SCRUM_TERMINOLOGY_STAGE_GATED_EXECUTION
    Claimed Scrum/Agile
    AND D03 Timebox Discipline >= 60
    AND D06 Handoff Reliance >= 60
    AND D08 External Gating >= 60

KANBAN_BOARD_WITHOUT_FLOW_CONTROL
    Claimed Kanban
    AND CORE-016 high
    AND (CORE-017 low OR CORE-018 low)
    AND D04 Flow/Pull Discipline < 55

PROJECTIZED_AGILE
    Claimed Agile/Scrum/Scaled Agile
    AND D01 Plan Commitment >= 65
    AND D08 External Gating >= 60
    AND D12 Product Orientation < 50

QA_GATED_DELIVERY
    D06 Handoff Reliance >= 60
    AND D07 Quality Integration < 50
    AND QA DownstreamGate high where QA module evidence is available

RESOURCE_POOL_DEVELOPMENT
    D05 Cross-Functional Ownership < 50
    AND DEV ResourcePoolBehavior high where developer evidence is available

SCRUM_MASTER_AS_PROJECT_CONTROL
    SM ProjectControlSubstitution high
    OR SM RoleOverlap high combined with low D10 Team Autonomy

PRODUCT_OWNER_WITHOUT_PRIORITY_AUTHORITY
    PROD PriorityAuthority low
    AND PROD ConstrainedAuthority high

CEREMONIES_WITHOUT_EMPIRICISM
    D03 Timebox Discipline >= 60
    AND D09 Empirical Learning < 45

FLOW_WITHOUT_PRODUCT_ORIENTATION
    D04 Flow/Pull Discipline >= 65
    AND D12 Product Orientation < 45

INTERRUPT_DRIVEN_PSEUDO_RESPONSIVENESS
    D02 Change Responsiveness high
    BUT D04 Flow/Pull Discipline low
    AND D09 Empirical Learning low
    AND unplanned-work evidence high when available

5. Cross-Functional Ownership
6. Handoff Reliance
7. External Gating

- accessible table equivalent,

- small-multiple role profile charts or horizontal bars,
- not one misleading composite role radar.

A role radar MAY be offered where all displayed axes are semantically comparable and the textual/table equivalent remains primary for accessibility.

Recommendation Principles
=========================

Recommendations MUST target observable constraints.

Example:

Instead of:
    "Adopt Scrum."

Prefer:
    "Quality work currently enters a downstream queue after development. If shorter feedback cycles are desired, test bringing QA/test design into refinement and moving verification into the team's normal completion criteria."

Instead of:
    "Remove project managers."

Prefer:
    "The project-management role is providing cross-team dependency coordination but also assigning daily team work. Consider separating governance/dependency coordination from team execution decisions and test whether the team can own the latter."

The assessment MUST NOT assume one methodology is universally superior.

Psychometric / Validation Plan
==============================

Before treating SDRA as a validated instrument:

1. Expert content review
    Include practitioners experienced in Scrum, Kanban/flow, Waterfall/stage-gate, product delivery, QA, engineering leadership, and project management.

2. Cognitive interviews
    Verify respondents interpret behavior items consistently across roles.

3. Pilot administration
    Use organizations/teams with diverse known operating patterns.

4. Reliability analysis
    Analyze candidate dimensions only where internal consistency is theoretically appropriate.
    Do not use Cronbach's alpha as automatic proof of validity.

5. Structural analysis
    Test whether the proposed 12-dimension structure is supported.
    Revise items/dimensions if empirical structure differs.

6. Known-groups / convergent evidence
    Compare profiles with independently characterized operating environments without using the methodology self-label as ground truth.

7. Inter-rater analysis
    Study role-cohort agreement/disagreement.

8. Classification calibration
    Re-estimate prototype vectors and thresholds from evidence.
    Do not freeze the pilot vectors merely because they are implemented.

9. Fairness / invariance review
    Test wording and scale behavior across role categories, organization sizes, languages, and relevant administration contexts.

10. Longitudinal sensitivity
    Determine whether the instrument detects genuine operating-model change rather than terminology change.

Versioning
==========

The following MUST be independently versioned:

- ItemBankVersion
- DimensionModelVersion
- RoleModelVersion
- ArchetypePrototypeVersion
- ClassificationRuleVersion
- PatternFlagRuleVersion
- ReportDefinitionVersion

A later scoring/classification model MUST NOT silently rewrite an original formal result.

Re-analysis under a newer model is a separately labeled derived interpretation.

Signal Architecture Requirements Exercised
==========================================

SDRA is intentionally a difficult Signal battery.

It exercises:

- multi-assessment battery composition,
- conditional role modules,
- non-PII role cohorting,
- multi-rater aggregation,
- privacy thresholding,
- incremental aggregation,
- custom/built-in scoring,
- derived classifications,
- multiple simultaneous result shapes,
- claimed-versus-observed comparison,
- lineage/explainability,
- semantic compatibility,
- radar visualization,
- accessible visualization equivalents,
- longitudinal comparison,
- report snapshots,
- analysis across respondent-role cohorts.

F#/Ordo owns:
- item/domain meaning,
- scoring,
- dimension aggregation,
- archetype similarity,
- pattern flags,
- coverage,
- privacy eligibility,
- comparison/congruence semantics.

Limen owns only browser mechanisms/rendering.

Aegis handles unexpected operational failures at external boundaries and does not replace typed assessment/domain outcomes.

======================

V1 is a candidate instrument.
